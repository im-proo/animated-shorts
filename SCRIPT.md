# "The Day a Drill Ate a Lake" — Original 62s Short Script

Topic: Lake Peigneur Disaster (November 20, 1980, Iberia Parish, Louisiana)
Facts: Texaco oil rig accidentally drilled into Diamond Crystal salt mine beneath 10ft shallow lake. Freshwater dissolved the salt, creating a massive sinkhole whirlpool. Sucked down rig, 11 barges, tugboat, 65 acres. Delcambre Canal reversed flow from Gulf of Mexico creating a 150ft waterfall. 0 deaths (all 55 miners + 3 fishermen escaped). Days later, 9 of 11 barges popped back up like corks due to compressed air pockets.

---

A tiny fourteen-inch drill bit accidentally drained an entire lake, swallowed an oil rig, and made an ocean flow backwards.
It’s 1980 in Louisiana, and Lake Peigneur is a peaceful ten-foot-deep lake where locals fish for bass.
Out in the water, an oil crew is drilling for crude, while thirteen hundred feet directly beneath them, fifty miners are working in a massive salt mine.
The drillers get their map coordinates slightly wrong.
Their fourteen-inch drill bit punches right through the roof of the salt mine.
At first, it’s just a trickle.
A normal leak stays the same size, but this mine is made of solid salt.
Fresh water melts salt like sugar in hot coffee.
Within hours, that tiny hole dissolves into a roaring whirlpool that sucks down the entire five-million-dollar oil platform, eleven barges, and sixty-five acres of trees.
Luckily, the lake was completely empty.
Unluckily, the Gulf of Mexico was not.
The lake bed dropped so low that the canal connected to the ocean reversed direction.
An ocean literally flowed backwards for days, creating a temporary hundred-and-fifty-foot waterfall to refill the hole.
Think of pulling the plug on a bathtub, except the drain dissolves the tub, and the plumbing eats the house.
Miraculously, all fifty miners escaped in time.
And two days later, underground air pressure burped—popping nine of the sunken barges right back to the surface like rubber ducks.
